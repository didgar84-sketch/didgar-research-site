Systematic reviews and PRISMA: a practical workflow

Plan a review protocol, reproducible search, screening, evidence extraction and PRISMA reporting. Keep reporting quality distinct from study validity.

Question and protocol

Where appropriate, define population, intervention or exposure, comparison and outcome. Specify eligibility, sources, screening and risk-of-bias methods before examining results. Protocol registration depends on the topic and registry requirements.

Search and screening

Record reproducible search strings, execution dates and databases. Deduplicate records, screen titles and abstracts, then assess full texts against predefined criteria. Document reasons for full-text exclusion.

Extraction and appraisal

Record design, sample, instruments, effect estimates and limitations. Reporting quality and risk of bias are distinct. Select appraisal tools suitable for the study design. Quantitative synthesis requires a defensible comparison of questions and methods.

Reporting

PRISMA is reporting guidance, not a quality certificate or a replacement for review methods. Report the flow diagram, full search strategies, study characteristics and limitations. Keep conclusions within the evidence.

Choose the review type

Systematic, scoping and narrative reviews have different purposes and methods. Match the question to evidence volume and team resources before starting. Mapping concepts differs from estimating an intervention effect; do not select the label afterward merely for appeal.

Check extraction and synthesis

Pilot the extraction form and define its fields. Explain conflict resolution and team roles in screening and extraction. Check units, direction and design before combining estimates. The availability of meta-analysis software does not justify pooling every study.

Preserve the review record

Retain the protocol, full searches, flow diagram, study table, bias assessment and synthesis rationale. Record update dates and added or removed studies. Explain the consequences of language or full-text restrictions.

Worked case and implementation decisions

The following is a fictional teaching case. Do not use its numbers or wording as actual study findings.

For a fictional active-learning review, define the question and eligibility before a reproducible search. Illustrative accounting: 120 records, 20 duplicates, 100 screened, 15 full texts assessed, five studies included and ten full-text exclusions with reasons. Replace these synthetic counts with actual records and distinguish record, report and study: one study can have multiple reports. PRISMA guides reporting; using its flow diagram alone does not validate searching, eligibility or risk-of-bias assessment.
