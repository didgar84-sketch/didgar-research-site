Verifying a new reporting standard: the PRISMA-trAIce case

Distinguish a familiar name, a published proposal and official endorsement using a documented 2026 case and a provenance-check workflow.

Separate three claims

A published paper can offer a useful proposal while not being an official extension of another standard. A familiar name, citation count or checklist appearance does not establish the original organization’s endorsement.

Specify whether you mean a published proposal, an endorsed extension or a journal requirement. Each label needs different evidence.

The current PRISMA-trAIce case

The letter PRISMA-trAIce: A Name Without Endorsement, published 18 August 2026, objects to presenting the proposal as an endorsed PRISMA checklist. This site does not label it an officially endorsed extension.

That objection does not establish that all AI-reporting suggestions are useless or all tools prohibited. Check PRISMA’s official extension list and organizational statements when using the guidance.

A five-step verification workflow

Find the title, DOI and original text. Identify document type, authors, date and corrections. Examine the claimed development and consensus process. Compare the official organization’s list and responses. Record actual journal or institutional requirements separately.

Report conflicting evidence with dates and precise claims. Absence from a list does not disprove every scientific contribution, but an official-endorsement label requires positive evidence.

Practical AI reporting

Without relying on an uncertain label, describe the tool’s actual role in search, screening, extraction, code or writing. Record tool/version, instructions and inputs, human controls and observed failures as appropriate.

Use valid base reporting guidance and the outlet’s policy. A tool cannot be an accountable author; researchers remain responsible for sources, privacy and results.

Keep a standard-selection log

Store name, organization, version, scope, source link, endorsement type, check date and applicability rationale. Date corrections if status changes.

Do not combine unrelated checklists to imply quality. Reporting should connect to actual design and decisions; an unsupported standards label does not establish scientific authority.
