Turnitin vs iThenticate

Key differences: Turnitin is widely used in academic coursework contexts, while iThenticate is designed for manuscripts and publication screening. Tips on uploading, limits and result analysis.

Interpreting similarity reports

The report should include overall percentage, matched sources list, highlighted segments and annotation about the type of match (quoted text, common phrases, prior self-publication).

Reduction strategies and rewriting

Patterns for rewriting, correct referencing, paraphrasing strategies and documentation to reduce similarity percentage.

Similarity requires human interpretation

Scores depend on settings, matching databases and document length. Correct quotations, references and common phrases may match. The aim is accurate attribution and original scholarly expression, not hiding matches or targeting an arbitrary percentage. No automated tool guarantees complete originality.

  • Record tool, date and report settings
  • Distinguish valid quotations from unattributed reuse
  • Cite original sources and improve the argument structure
  • Respect confidentiality when uploading manuscripts

A scholarly revision workflow

Identify whether a match comes from references, common language, a valid quotation or unattributed reuse. For unattributed ideas, swapping a few words is insufficient: express your understanding and cite the source. Retain exact language only with appropriate quotation and citation.

Why settings matter

Excluding references or quotations, setting minimum match size and selecting comparison databases may change the score. Compare versions with equivalent settings. Translation and database limitations affect coverage. A low score does not rule out fabricated references, invented data or weak reasoning.

Before uploading confidential manuscripts, check authorization and service retention policy. Commercial tool access must be lawful and agreed. This website itself is not Turnitin or iThenticate.

Worked case and implementation decisions

The following is a fictional teaching case. Do not use its numbers or wording as actual study findings.

Read a similarity report to assess attribution and wording, not to find a magic percentage. In the fictional case, references and correctly attributed quotations raise the score, while one unattributed paragraph also appears among matches. Record settings, the submitted version and the source of each match. Database coverage, translation and unindexed material can hide issues. Changing words merely to reduce a percentage does not repair idea attribution or authorship problems.
