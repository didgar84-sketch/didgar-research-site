Thesis by publication: linking chapters and an integrated argument

Build a coherent thesis around published work, connecting a central question, linking chapters, author contributions and an integrated discussion.

Begin with institutional rules

A publication thesis is more than a folder of accepted papers. Institutions differ on eligible outputs, status, number, joint authorship, research period and allowed versions. Preserve the applicable guidance and program decision before planning.

ANU illustrates an integrated argument with linking sections, not automatic permission for your university. This guide sets no universal minimum paper count.

Design the central argument

Write an overarching question and assign each paper a role. Measurement, association and experience studies could inform one hypothetical argument; unrelated papers do not become coherent because they share an author.

Map problem, paper contribution, data, method, actual finding and remaining uncertainty. An integrated introduction and review establish common concepts and theory.

A completed linking paragraph

Hypothetical example: “The preceding chapter examined the operational definition of engagement. This chapter studies its association with learning-support use in a limited sample, addressing the second part of the central question. Sample and design differences limit coefficient comparison. The following chapter uses qualitative material to examine process.”

Ground the paragraph in actual neighboring chapters. Do not invent findings. A closing link should state how the paper changed the thesis argument and what remains open.

Integrate the discussion

Do not concatenate article discussions. Examine convergence, disagreement, differing samples and instruments, alternatives and the contribution of the thesis as a whole. An integration table can connect claims, evidence and limits.

Manage necessary repetition under institutional rules, identify article versions and explain expanded chapters or new analyses. Keep terminology, numbering and references coherent across the thesis.

Contributions and rights

Document your actual role and coauthor contributions to question, design, collection, analysis and writing. Obtain required approvals; first authorship alone does not explain contribution. Check article version, figure reuse and repository permissions.

Accurately label submitted, accepted, preprint and published outputs. Connect related thesis and article versions and resolve confidentiality or examination requirements with the institution.
