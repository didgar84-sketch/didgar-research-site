Similarity reports: components and revision checklist

Review report components, document versions and tool settings, and use a revision-action table. A similarity percentage alone cannot determine plagiarism.

Prepared by: Dr. Didgar Research Institute · Last revised: 2026-10-06 · 2 min read
Expected deliverables
  • Keep a match/reason/action matrix with the original report.
Decision workbook and example

In this guide

  • A reviewable report goes beyond a score

  • Sources and further reading

A comprehensive report must include the following sections:

  1. Executive summary — overall similarity percentage and recommended action
  2. Details of each matched source with reference/link
  3. Highlighted matching text with location/page references
  4. Recommended corrective actions and next steps
  5. Final assessment and recommendation for publication or revision

A reviewable report goes beyond a score

Identify the location, source and proposed action for each concern. Document version and date matter: an earlier report does not describe a revised manuscript. Do not declare misconduct solely from a percentage.

  • Document version and tool details
  • Matching table with location, source and use category
  • Prioritized revision plan with responsibilities
  • Human review against institutional policy

This is an educational example, not a completed report on your manuscript. Distinguish attribution problems, explainable similarity and issues requiring institutional judgment. A reviewer should not decide misconduct only from a tool’s color or score. Retain filename, version and date to track revisions.

Worked case and implementation decisions

The following is a fictional teaching case. Do not use its numbers or wording as actual study findings.

Two documents with the same percentage may have different problems. In fictional A, most matches are technical terms and references; in B, a complete unattributed argument appears. A useful summary describes match types and actions rather than declaring misconduct from the aggregate score. Any secondary view excluding references or quotations needs transparent settings and should not conceal the original report. The actual text, provenance, attribution and institutional policy determine the decision.
